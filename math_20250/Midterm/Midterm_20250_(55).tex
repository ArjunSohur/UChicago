1. (16 points) Give an example of each of the following, or explain why it is not possible.

\vskip.11in

a) Two non-square matrices that are row equivalent but not column equivalent.

\vskip1.5in

b) A real matrix with nullspace equal to $\text{span}\Big(\Big\{\begin{pmatrix} 
2\\3\\4\end{pmatrix}\Big\}\Big)$ and column space equal to $\text{span}\Big(\Big\{\begin{pmatrix} 
-1\\0\\2\end{pmatrix}\Big\}\Big)$ 
\vskip1.5in

c) A real invertible matrix $A$ such that $\det(A^2)=10\det(A)$
\vskip1.5in

d) Two distinct subspaces $U,V$ of $\mathbb{R}^4$ so that their intersection is a subspace of dimension 3.

3. (10 points) Complete the following:

\vskip.15in

a) State the Rank Theorem. Give an example that illustrates it.


\vskip3.5in

b) Give four examples of conditions that are equivalent to the following: the columns of an $n \times n$ matrix $A$ are linearly independent.

4. (12 points) Prove \textbf{one} of the following:

a) A square matrix $A$ is idempotent if it has the property that $A^2=A$. What are the possible values of the determinant of a real square idempotent matrix $A$? Prove your answer.

b) If a homogeneous system of linear equations over $\mathbb{R}$ has more variables than equations, then the system has infinitely many solutions.

c) Let $V$ be a vector space over a field $\mathbb{F}$. Let $c$ be an element of $\mathbb{F}$ and let $\mathbf{v}$ be an element
of $V$. Suppose that $c\mathbf{v} = 0$. Use the defining properties of a vector space to show that either $c = 0$ or $\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}$.


\newpage
\vskip.15in

5. (25 points) Complete \textbf{all} of the following. Always, sometimes, or never? If it is always or never true, give a proof. If it is sometimes true, give an example where it’s true and where it’s false.

\vskip.15in

a) If matrices $A$ and $B$ are row equivalent, then the matrix $A-B$ is row equivalent to each of them.

\vskip2.5in

b) A matrix that commutes with a diagonal matrix is also diagonal.





\vskip2.5in


c) If two distinct subspaces of $V$ have bases $B_1$ and $B_2$, then the set $B_1 \cup B_2$ is linearly independent.

\newpage

\vskip.15in

d) A linear transformation from $\mathbb{R}^8$ to $\mathbb{R}^5$ is onto.

\vskip3.5in

e) A set of three distinct vectors in a vector space $V$ forms a basis for a subspace of $V$.

7. (6 points) Consider the function $S: \mathcal{P}_3 \to \mathcal{P}_6$ that takes each real cubic polynomial of degree at most 3 to its square (that is, to the polynomial times itself). Is this a linear transformation? Explain why or why not.

b) Show that vector space $(\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})^3$ has exactly 28 subspaces.

a) Give a basis for $\mathbb{R}^3$ where the vector $\begin{pmatrix}

    4\\2\\4

b) Compute the dimension of the smallest subspace of $M_{2 \times 2}^\mathbb{R}$ containing all of its invertible matrices.
